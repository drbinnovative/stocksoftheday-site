What happened

Disc Medicine announced topline results from APOLLO, a Phase 3 trial of its experimental drug bitopertin in erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP). EPP is a rare disease that causes severe pain and reactions when patients are exposed to sunlight. The company said the trial met both co-primary endpoints and all secondary endpoints.

The two co-primary endpoints were a reduction in a substance called protoporphyrin IX (PPIX) and more time in light without pain. The company reported a reduction in whole-blood metal-free PPIX from baseline of -41.4% versus placebo (p<0.0001). Average monthly total time in light without pain during month 6 rose by +12.2 hours versus placebo (p=0.0010).

The trial enrolled 183 patients and randomized them 1:1 to 60 mg of oral bitopertin or placebo once daily for 6 months. The company said bitopertin was generally well tolerated. Dizziness was the most common adverse event in the bitopertin group, at 50%. The company described it as mild or moderate and generally transient. It also said there were no adverse events above Grade 3.

Disc said it plans to resubmit a New Drug Application to the U.S. FDA by the end of 2026. It said an FDA decision is anticipated by mid-2027.
